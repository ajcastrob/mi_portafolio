# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Founders and small teams in LATAM who need a website or internal tool and cannot (or should not) hire a full product team. They arrive evaluating a freelancer, often on a phone, with a real project in mind. The job in the first 30 seconds is: decide if José is the person who can build something their team can keep, then write to him.

## Product Purpose

This is José Castro’s freelance site. It exists to start projects: a visitor sends a message (form, email, or WhatsApp) and a conversation begins. Craft, stack, and biography are evidence for that ask, not the destination. Success is a qualified inquiry, not a longer scroll.

The interface language is Spanish. José is a comunicador social and freelance developer; the promise is webs and tools people actually use and teams can maintain.

## Brand Personality

Directo, cercano, práctico.

Speak in first person, in specific Spanish. Name the work, the stack, and the offer. Handmade cues (polaroid, handwriting, offset chips) exist to feel like a person, not a studio. Closeness without cute; practical without cold. The visitor should feel they can write today and get a clear reply, not that they are touring a personal brand.

## Anti-references

- Generic dark “passionate developer” portfolios with a photo, a stack grid, and no point of view
- Glassmorphism, gradient text, and SaaS landing templates (hero metric, identical feature cards, tracked eyebrows on every section)
- Agency-slick motion reels that look expensive and unmaintainable
- Student-template energy that undercuts the hire: empty “About”, placeholder title, craft as costume

Keep the current polaroid / handwriting / offset-chip language. Do not replace it with editorial-magazine serif or terminal-native dark mode.

## Design Principles

1. **Contact is the product.** Every section should make writing easier, not just more impressive. The form, the promise of a reply, and the contact chips carry more weight than another paragraph of biography.
2. **Show the dual craft without splitting the brand.** Software and comunicación are one offer (“webs que tu equipo puede mantener”), not two columns competing for attention.
3. **Practice what you preach.** The site should feel maintainable: one voice, one system, no demo of every trick. If a flourish would be hard for a small team to keep, it does not belong here.
4. **Evidence, then the ask.** Projects exist to support the inquiry. Three real pieces beat a catalog. Descriptions should say what the thing does for a team, not which buzzwords it uses.
5. **A person you can hire.** Copy, photo, and material details should read as José, available in LATAM, not as a template wearing his name.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No extra bar beyond what the site already does. Do not regress: Spanish `lang`, visible focus, labeled form fields with errors, keyboard-capable nav, and `prefers-reduced-motion` where motion exists. Do not add a WCAG program as a design goal unless a later brief raises it.
